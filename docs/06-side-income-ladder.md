# Chapter 6: The Side-Income Ladder

A physicist plots an escape orbit; a pragmatic person climbs a ladder. The
side-income ladder is how you **earn before you leap**, so that the day you
leave bedside (or wherever you are) is a numbers decision, not a prayer.

## The rule above all rules

**Every rung of the ladder must pay in money, or in *proof*, or it is not a
rung.** A rung that only pays in "exposure" or "it would look impressive" is
a trap (but see the single exception, below).

## The ladder, bottom to top

| Rung | What it is | Typical pay | What it proves |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Free work for a real audience | $0 | you can finish and ship |
| 2 | Small one-off paid gigs | $50–300 each | a stranger values it |
| 3 | Repeatable product | $5–50 per sale | a market exists |
| 4 | Steady freelance/contract clients | $500–5,000/mo | you can hold demand |
| 5 | Full pivot (job or business) | your salary | you're in the new field |

**The single rung-1 exception:** one piece of free work is allowed — one
guest post or one free template, done quickly, for a *specific named person
or community you actually respect* — because at rung 1 you have no proof yet
and one free sample is your entry ticket. After one: rung 2 requires money.

## Maya's ladder in real life

- **Rung 1:** wrote a free "30-day AI-onboarding for night-shift nurses"
  guide for a nursing-education Discord she'd lurked in all year. 40
  downloads, 3 kind comments, 1 person asking "would you do this for our
  newsletter?"
- **Rung 2:** three little paid gigs: a clinic-podcast shownotes rewrite
  ($150), one training-doc edit ($200), a blog post for a health-tech
  startup ($250).
- **Rung 3:** packaged the guide as a $9 "print + fill-in" product on
  Gumroad. Sold 22 copies in three months. Tiny, *and* it's proof.
- **Rung 4 (in progress):** a two-clients-per-month retainer writing
  patient-education materials for a SaaS company.

Notice what she never did: quit first, buy a $2,000 course, or cold-email 200
recruiters. Structure first, courage funded by arithmetic.

## Time-boxing with a day job

Time is the real currency. Constraints make the ladder climbable:

- **3 hours/week of *building*** (portfolio, products) matters more than
  10 hours of *browsing*.
- One **weekly 25-minute "admin window"** — job applications, invoicing,
  follow-ups. Batching admin prevents the death-by-a-thousand-notifications.
- Your day job still has health insurance and a pension. Respect it while it
  pays for the glide.

## Honest numbers template (from the worksheet)

For each gig, log it: date, what, hours, pay, proof-it-created. At the end of
each month you can compute your **effective hourly rate** and your **proof
count**. When rung-4 income exceeds, say, 50–80% of your living-cost margin,
*then* you look at Chapter 7's leap math (a budget, not vibes).

**Not financial, tax, or legal advice.** Invoicing, self-employment taxes,
and business entities vary wildly by country and state. Ask a qualified
professional before the money gets real. (If and when you form a company
of your own, you'll get to pick a name and file it — Dylan did that too,
hence "Asdale Dynamics, LLC" — but this handbook deliberately stays out of
the forms-and-fees details.)
