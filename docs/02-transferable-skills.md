# Chapter 2: Inventorying Your Transferable Skills

Here is the best-kept secret of career pivots: **you are already carrying a
toolkit**. The problem is that old-job skills are labelled in old-job
language, so strangers can't see them until you translate them.

A transferable skills inventory is the act of renaming your experience in the
language of the field you're entering. It is the single highest-value hour in
this entire handbook.

## The three-bucket method

For each *thing you actually did* at work (not job titles — actions), sort it:

1. **Direct transfer** — the new field needs exactly this, under the exact
   same name.
2. **Translated transfer** — the new field needs the underlying skill, under
   a different name. You rename it.
3. **Context, not skill** — genuinely specific to the old field. Don't lead
   with it; mention it as texture later.

## Maya's inventory (a slice)

What Maya did at the bedside → what she *also* did, in writing-world language:

| I did this at work | Bucket | New-world label |
|---|---|---|
| Ran family-meetings after a terrible night | Translated | facilitated difficult stakeholder conversations |
| Taught a confused elderly patient their med schedule in 4 minutes | Translated | explained complex systems to distracted audiences |
| Wrote post-fall huddle protocols twice | Direct | procedural/technical writing |
| Kept crash-cart checks at 100% for 3 years | Translated | process auditing & compliance documentation |
| Ran the shared unit spreadsheet for supplies | Translated | lightweight data tracking & reporting |
| Started an IV in the dark, halfway through a bad night | Context | (great at parties; not on the résumé) |

Notice the last row. Nursing *context* — emergencies, night shifts — is
valuable background that makes her stories vivid in interviews. It's in her
**conversation**, not her **bullet points**.

## How to do it (45-minute protocol)

1. Open [`docs/worksheets/skills-inventory.md`](worksheets/skills-inventory.md).
2. List **20 things** you did in the last 5–10 years that somebody was glad
   you did. Use verbs. "Taught," "fixed," "organized," "wrote," "calmed."
3. Bucket each one: Direct / Translated / Context.
4. For every Translated item, force yourself to write the new-world label in
   one to four words.
5. Star the five items you're proudest of. Those drive your résumé (Ch. 3),
   your portfolio (Ch. 4), and your stories in interviews.

## The AI assist (optional, honest)

A free AI chatbot can help you brainstorm *labels* after you've supplied the
*honest facts*. Never let it invent the facts — it will happily invent your
brilliant, fictional life. Bring it your list of what you actually did and
ask: *"Given the field I'm entering [], what would each be called there?
Which are strongest?"* Its suggestions are your draft labels — you confirm
each one is true.

## Adapt it to you

Any field has all three buckets. Retail → you have inventory systems, de-
escalation, merchandising analytics. Classrooms → curriculum design and
group management. Warehouses → logistics software fluency. Hospitals → the
whole unseen world of clinical documentation and mentoring. The language
changes; the skills are yours and they leave with you.
