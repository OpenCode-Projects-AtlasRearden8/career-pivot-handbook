# Chapter 3: Résumé Rewrites with AI Help

A pivot résumé has one job: make a stranger believe you might already be
partly the person they need. AI tools can help you get there — *if* you stay
the editor-in-chief and stay honest.

## The layout that works for pivots

Swap the default résumé order. Most people use: Education → Work Experience →
Skills. A pivot résumé leads with **what you can do**:

1. **Header** — name, bridge line, email/LinkedIn/GitHub.
2. **Summary (2–3 lines)** — your pivot in your own words.
3. **Portfolio / Selected work** — 2–3 links (Chapter 4 makes these real).
4. **Relevant skills** — the "direct + translated" wins, named in new-world
   language.
5. **Work experience** — bullets re-translated using your Chapter 2 inventory.
6. **Certifications / education** — short, at the end.

### Maya's bridge line

Old résumé: "Registered Nurse, St. Hazel Hospital, 2014–present."

New header:

> **Maya Nurse** — Technical & clarity writer (healthcare → tech)
> Portfolio: mayanurse.github.io — Email: may@example.com

The bridge line doesn't hide her past — healthcare is her *differentiator* —
it just leads with where she's going.

## Using AI for the actual rewrite (free tools)

**Tools:** any free chat AI (a local model via [Ollama](https://ollama.com),
or a free web chatbot) works. Nothing here needs a paid plan.

**The good loop:**

1. Paste a real bullet from your old job. Ask: *"Rewrite for a writing and
   documentation role. Keep only facts I've included; don't invent metrics."*
2. Compare three rewrites. Cringe at the artificial "spearheaded synergy"
   ones. Keep the plain, concrete one.
3. Replace vague words with your own true numbers. "Improved some charting
   times" → "cut average med-reconciliation time from 12 to 7 minutes"
   (only if genuinely true and non-identifying).

**The honesty rules (non-negotiable):**

- AI drafts structure and wording. **You supply all facts.**
- If a rewritten bullet contains something you can't say out loud with a
  straight face while looking at your own reflection — delete it.
- Never paste real patient information, your employer's internal docs, or
  anything identifying into any AI tool. Use placeholders.

## One résumé per target (the unglamorous truth)

One master résumé → three tailored variants (writer at a health-tech company;
documentation generalist; content/education role). Each variant links a
*different* portfolio item on top. Fifteen minutes each, not three hours.

### Application-robot reality

Many large employers filter with software (ATS) that prefers simple, plain
résumés. So: a standard font, no tables or graphics, standard section names
("Work Experience"), and file as a PDF *named well*
(`maya-nurse-writer-resume.pdf`). Then, for humans: a polite short note that
links your portfolio.
