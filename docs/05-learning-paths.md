# Chapter 5: Affordable Learning Paths

You don't need a $15,000 certificate to change careers. You need **the
minimum dose that gets you producing**, plus free resources you actually
finish. Certificates create feelings of progress; *making things* creates
progress.

## Maya's rule: never "study" when you could "produce with support"

Maya caught herself about to sign up for a professional certification in
"Emerging Technologies." Instead she asked: *what's the cheapest way to get
the smallest amount of understanding I need to produce my next portfolio
piece?* That reframing saved her six months and a lot of money.

## The affordability ladder, from free to paid

### Tier 1 — Free (start here)

- **Documentation and user guides.** The boring, patient AI documentation of
  tools like Ollama, GitHub, and VS Code covers most of what you need.
- **Libraries.** The public library, free of charge, has business books,
  plus lending stops (Libby) and (in many cities) free tech intros.
- **Official community tutorials.** GitHub's own "Hello World," the Markdown
  Guide, W3Schools for the basics. Slow, steady, free forever.
- **YouTube deep-dives, chosen carefully:** search tutorials, watch
  picture-in-picture with your laptop, *build along*, and take ten skimmy
  notes. Total cost: the occasional ad.

### Tier 2 — Nearly free

- **One focused e-book per topic** — a $10–20 book beats a
  $1,200 course at the beginner stage because books move at *your* pace. (This
  is why Dylan Asdale writes these things.)
- **One month of one skill platform, with a real deadline,** then cancel.

### Tier 3 — Paid, chosen carefully

- **Community college extension courses** ($150–500, evening hours) —
  excellent for skills like data basics, photography, medical coding, etc.
- **Short, honed courses from a specific practitioner** whose free content
  you've already benefitted from — buy after reading their free stuff.

**Burn-rate guardrails:**

- Total spend on learning before your first paid pivot-dollar: **under
  $300** (books and a single community-college class). Everything above that
  number waits until a real client/customer (Chapter 6) validates the path.
- No "career coaching" packages over $250. A good alumni-network coffee,
  or this handbook, covers the same ground for less.

## Structure a self-taught "semester" (12 weeks, ~3 h/week)

| Weeks | Focus | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Tool fundamentals (pick 2 tools from your field) | everyday-comfort, template made |
| 4–6 | Vocabulary of the new field | 1 explainer post |
| 7–9 | Bridge skill (Ch. 2's starred Translated items) | 1 redesign/analysis piece |
| 10–12 | Mini-project end-to-end | 1 polished portfolio item |

That's it. That's a real, respectable "semester" of self-directed
re-skilling. Costs: $0–30.

## The ADHD-friendly operating manual (low-energy productivity)

This applies whether or not you have ADHD — pivots are stressful, and even
your best brain gets tired.

- **10-minute doors.** If you can only write "put the title on the page" —
  that counts. Momentum > plan quality.
- **Body doubling.** Study with another human (in-person, or a focus-app's
  shared sessions). You're less likely to abandon a boring chunk when another
  person is quietly doing theirs.
- **Decision hygiene.** Pre-decide *next session's exact first action* before
  closing the laptop tonight ("open file; write heading"), so tomorrow-you
  starts dumb and easy.
- **Visual timers** and one shared annual view of your 12-week semester. If
  you fall behind a week, don't punish; just flex the plateau into week 13.
